Reading routes

Explore by theme. Each book can be read independently.

These groups offer different ways into the country selections below. They are not reading sequences: each book can be read independently. Categories overlap, and a title may appear in more than one group. Select a book title to search for it on Goodreads.

Expansive social worlds

Novels that build a whole social environment through interconnected lives, institutions and relationships.

  • Middlemarch — George Eliot
  • The Maias — Eça de Queirós
  • The Makioka Sisters — Jun'ichirō Tanizaki
  • A Fine Balance — Rohinton Mistry
Interior lives

Fiction centred on private thought, conflicted motives, self-deception and feelings that remain unspoken.

  • Kokoro — Natsume Sōseki
  • Doctor Glas — Hjalmar Söderberg
  • Zeno's Conscience — Italo Svevo
  • The Remains of the Day — Kazuo Ishiguro
Narrative invention

Books that reshape the act of storytelling through unusual structures, invented realities or self-conscious play.

  • The Invention of Morel — Adolfo Bioy Casares
  • If on a winter's night a traveler — Italo Calvino
  • Dictionary of the Khazars — Milorad Pavić
  • Ferdydurke — Witold Gombrowicz
History through ordinary lives

Large historical changes experienced through homes, work, family relationships and everyday decisions.

  • The Time of the Doves — Mercè Rodoreda
  • Things Fall Apart — Chinua Achebe
  • To Live — Yu Hua
  • Palace Walk — Naguib Mahfouz
Families across generations

Family histories that explore inheritance, loyalty and the persistence of the past across generations.

  • Buddenbrooks — Thomas Mann
  • One Hundred Years of Solitude — Gabriel García Márquez
  • Homegoing — Yaa Gyasi
  • The Eighth Life (for Brilka) — Nino Haratischwili
Love, marriage and desire

Relationships shaped by longing, unequal power, social expectations and the distance between intimacy and understanding.

  • Anna Karenina — Leo Tolstoy
  • Some Prefer Nettles — Jun'ichirō Tanizaki
  • Chéri — Colette
  • The Tortoise and the Hare — Elizabeth Jenkins
Leaving, returning and belonging

Stories of migration, exile and homecoming, where home becomes a question rather than a settled place.

  • The Emigrants — Vilhelm Moberg
  • The Lonely Londoners — Samuel Selvon
  • Americanah — Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie
  • By the Sea — Abdulrazak Gurnah
Growing into a changing world

Coming-of-age novels in which education, family and social expectations shape an emerging sense of self.

  • A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man — James Joyce
  • Nervous Conditions — Tsitsi Dangarembga
  • Annie John — Jamaica Kincaid
  • This Earth of Mankind — Pramoedya Ananta Toer
Power, institutions and moral compromise

Fiction examining how authority enters daily life, and what accommodation, resistance or complicity can cost.

  • The Palace of Dreams — Ismail Kadare
  • Waiting for the Barbarians — J. M. Coetzee
  • The Censor's Notebook — Liliana Corobca
  • The President — Miguel Ángel Asturias
Satire and dark humour

Books that use irony, absurdity or grotesque situations to expose social habits and human contradictions.

  • The Good Soldier Švejk — Jaroslav Hašek
  • The Time Regulation Institute — Ahmet Hamdi Tanpınar
  • The Posthumous Memoirs of Brás Cubas — Machado de Assis
  • Broken Glass — Alain Mabanckou
Myth, folklore and the fantastic

Narratives in which mythic, supernatural or fable-like elements coexist with the material world.

  • The Famished Road — Ben Okri
  • Sleepwalking Land — Mia Couto
  • The Man with the Compound Eyes — Wu Ming-yi
  • The Blue Fox — Sjón
Land, landscape and survival

Stories in which the natural world, rural work or attachment to a place actively shapes human life.

  • Independent People — Halldór Laxness
  • The Tree of Man — Patrick White
  • Barren Lives — Graciliano Ramos
  • Deep Rivers — José María Arguedas
Short and concentrated

Novels and novellas that create a substantial emotional or conceptual world within a relatively compact form.

  • The Tunnel — Ernesto Sábato
  • Men in the Sun — Ghassan Kanafani
  • The Invention of Morel — Adolfo Bioy Casares
  • The Wedding of Zein — Tayeb Salih
Memory, grief and the passing of time

Fiction about loss and recollection, where revisiting the past changes the shape of the present.

  • The Sea — John Banville
  • The Stone Angel — Margaret Laurence
  • To the Lighthouse — Virginia Woolf
  • The Summer Book — Tove Jansson
Mystery, uncertainty and suspicion

Literary fiction that borrows the tensions of crime and mystery while keeping perception and certainty in question.

  • The Darkroom of Damocles — W. F. Hermans
  • The Pledge — Friedrich Dürrenmatt
  • Blackwater — Kerstin Ekman
  • The Sound of Things Falling — Juan Gabriel Vásquez
Faith, doubt and spiritual searching

Books that examine belief through moral conflict, estrangement, devotion or the difficulty of finding meaning.

  • Barabbas — Pär Lagerkvist
  • Death and the Dervish — Meša Selimović
  • Gilead — Marilynne Robinson
  • Niels Lyhne — Jens Peter Jacobsen
